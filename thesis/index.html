B.Sc. thesis · Daffodil International University · 2026

Reading paper ECGs from a phone photo.

In many hospitals in Bangladesh, 12-lead ECGs are printed on thermal paper and handed to the patient; no digital signal is kept. A phone photograph is often the only copy. We built a dataset of 1,191 smartphone photographs of paper ECGs from Enam Medical College Hospital (1,175 patients), each labelled from the cardiologist's own written remark, and a strict evaluation protocol around it. A deliberately plain image model reads the photo directly and reaches a macro AUROC of 0.923 (95% CI 0.895–0.946) on a locked test set opened once.

The problem

Most ECG AI needs a signal. Here, there is only paper.

No digital signal

Paper printouts keep no signal, and most ECG models need one.

Messy photos

Phone, light, tilt, curl, shadows and handwriting on the page all vary.

Few clinic photos

Existing image datasets are mostly scans or renders; few are phone photos from real South-Asian clinics.

The contribution is the dataset and the evaluation protocol, not a fancy model. The model is plain on purpose, so the results measure the data and the protocol.

Five labels (multi-label)

  • Normal
  • Ischaemia
  • Arrhythmia/conduction block
  • Rate abnormality
  • Other

Each handwritten remark was mapped to classes with a published vocabulary (AHA/ACCF/HRS 2007 statements, PTB-XL codes); rate abnormality comes from the printed heart rate (<60 or >100). The split was frozen before training: 949 development records (5-fold cross-validation by patient) and 240 test records, opened exactly once and enforced by code.

What careful data work found

  1. Duplicates. 10 pairs of the same printout had been entered twice — merged.
  2. Silent imputation. The clinical spreadsheet had quietly filled blanks with imputed values — restored to missing.
  3. Clipped values. ECG values were clipped (exactly 8 records at the minimum and maximum of every field) — 40 cells corrected by reading the printed header from the photos (98–99% agreement with known values).
  4. Readable text. 22% of images carried printed or handwritten text — masked so the model cannot read it.

Pre-registered

What did not work

Negative results, reported as planned.

  • Higher image resolution

    No measurable gain.

  • Grid rectification

    Flattening the paper geometrically: +0.011, interval includes 0.

  • Photo-quality scores

    Measured blur and lighting do not predict errors.

  • Lab values from the ECG photo

    Anaemia and kidney function: not better than age + sex.

Where this goes

Next steps

  • Blinded reader study

    Two cardiologists rate reports from four systems (protocol drafted).

  • Prospective test set

    150–300 new photos for paper 2.

  • Controlled-access release

    Public release of the dataset once permissions are final.

  • Offline phone app

    Photo in, report and a “refer” flag out — for clinics without digital ECG.

  • Bigger models, more sites

    Fine-tuning a large ECG vision-language model; better digitisers; more hospitals and phone types.

  • ECG age (exploratory)

    An “ECG age” from the photo: +5.6 years in patients with reduced kidney function. Early; not in the papers.
